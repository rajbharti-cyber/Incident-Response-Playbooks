# 🚨 Incident Severity Classification Standard

## 📌 Purpose

The purpose of this document is to establish a standardized approach for classifying information security incidents based on business impact, operational disruption, regulatory exposure, and organizational risk.

This classification framework supports:
- Consistent incident handling
- Governance oversight
- Escalation management
- Regulatory readiness
- Operational resilience

---

# 🎯 Severity Classification Objectives

- Standardize incident prioritization
- Improve escalation efficiency
- Support governance reporting
- Enable faster response coordination
- Reduce operational confusion during incidents

---

# 📊 Incident Severity Matrix

| Severity | Business Impact | Example Incidents | Response Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Critical | Enterprise-wide disruption | Ransomware outbreak, major data breach | Immediate |
| High | Significant operational impact | Unauthorized access, privileged compromise | < 1 hour |
| Medium | Limited business disruption | Phishing incident, malware alert | Same business day |
| Low | Minimal operational impact | Isolated suspicious activity | 24–72 hours |

---

# 🛡️ Severity Determination Factors

Incident severity should consider:
- Data sensitivity
- Number of affected systems
- Regulatory implications
- Financial exposure
- Operational downtime
- Customer impact
- Reputational risk

---

# 📋 Escalation Requirements

## Critical Incidents
Require immediate escalation to:
- Security leadership
- Executive management
- Legal/compliance teams
- Incident response management

---

## High Severity Incidents
Require:
- Accelerated investigation
- Formal management notification
- Enhanced monitoring

---

## Medium & Low Incidents
Require:
- Standard triage
- Documentation
- Risk-based prioritization
